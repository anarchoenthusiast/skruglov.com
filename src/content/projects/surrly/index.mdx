Surrly is a solo-built iOS dream journal powered by AI. I started with a simple product thesis: dreams are easy to lose, hard to describe, and often too personal for a rigid journaling tool. The product needed to feel calm, private, and immediate — a gentle place to capture a dream before it fades.

I took Surrly from idea to launch over three months in the summer of 2025. As the builder, I owned the full loop: product direction, UX, interface design, brand, copywriting, AI interpretation flows, iOS implementation, landing page, and TestFlight release. There was no handoff between strategy, design, and engineering — every decision had to survive contact with the actual product.

The project was also a test of my AI-assisted building workflow. I used AI across research synthesis, interface copy exploration, implementation support, and iteration speed — not as a replacement for product judgment, but as a way to compress the path from idea to working software.

The main challenge was making AI feel supportive rather than intrusive. I shaped the experience around a soft journaling flow, lightweight interpretation, and a visual tone that sits between sleep and wakefulness. Nothing harsh, nothing clinical — just enough structure to help people record, revisit, and better understand their dreams.

For me, Surrly is a compact example of how I build: finding a product angle, designing the system around it, writing the interface, implementing the app, and shipping something real without waiting for a larger team.
